export const title = "Invariant checking through search";

Before reaching for a full temporal logic, notice how much verification plain `search` already buys you. An *invariant* is a property that holds in every reachable state; its violation is a reachable state where it fails. So to check an invariant, search for a violation:

```maude
mod VENDING is
  sorts Coin Item Marking .
  subsorts Coin Item < Marking .
  op __ : Marking Marking -> Marking [assoc comm id: null] .
  op null : -> Marking .
  ops $ q : -> Coin [ctor] .
  ops apple cake : -> Item [ctor] .
  rl [buy-cake]  : $ => cake .
  rl [buy-apple] : $ => apple q .
  rl [change]    : q q q q => $ .
endm

search $ q q q =>* cake cake M:Marking .
```

`No solution.` is the verdict: starting from a dollar and three quarters, *no reachable state* contains two cakes — a dollar and change can never buy two of them. The search explored the entire reachable state space (six states here) and proved a universal property.

When the invariant *fails*, the search gives you the witness for free:

```maude
mod VENDING is
  sorts Coin Item Marking .
  subsorts Coin Item < Marking .
  op __ : Marking Marking -> Marking [assoc comm id: null] .
  op null : -> Marking .
  ops $ q : -> Coin [ctor] .
  ops apple cake : -> Item [ctor] .
  rl [buy-cake]  : $ => cake .
  rl [buy-apple] : $ => apple q .
  rl [change]    : q q q q => $ .
endm

search $ q q q =>* apple q q q q M:Marking .
show path 1 .
```

Claim to refute: "you can never hold an apple and four quarters at once". A solution turns up immediately, and `show path` replays how to get there — a counterexample trace, exactly what you want from a failed verification.

Two practical notes. First, negative properties are often phrased with a condition: `search init =>* C:Conf such that bad(C) .` where `bad` is an equationally-defined predicate — this is how you express invariants that aren't a simple pattern. Second, this method needs the reachable state space to be finite (search runs forever otherwise, and the Cancel button becomes relevant); for infinite-state systems, bounded search (`search [1, 50] …` caps the depth — bounded model checking) or *equational abstraction* (adding equations that collapse states into finitely many equivalence classes) recover decidability.

For properties about ongoing behaviour — orderings of events, "always eventually" — patterns over single states aren't enough. That's what the LTL model checker in chapter 13 is for.
